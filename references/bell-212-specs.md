# Bell 212 (UH-1N Twin Huey) — Technical Reference

## Overview

The Bell 212 is a twin-engine, medium utility helicopter developed from the Bell 205 (UH-1H). It entered commercial service in 1971 and military service as the UH-1N. Approximately 900 were produced. The aircraft used in The Matrix (1999) was a real Bell 212 filmed in Sydney, Australia.

## Powerplant

- Engine: Pratt & Whitney Canada PT6T-3 (or -3B) "Twin-Pac"
- Configuration: Two coupled PT6A-34 turboshaft engines sharing a single combining gearbox
- Combined power: 1,800 shp (1,342 kW) total; derated to ~1,290 shp for normal operations
- Key safety feature: If one engine fails, the combining gearbox automatically commands the surviving engine to develop full power. The aircraft is capable of single-engine flight at max takeoff weight.

## Rotor System

- Main rotor: 2-blade semi-rigid "seesaw" configuration
- Rotor RPM (power on): 97–100% NR (314–324 RPM); 100% = 324 RPM
- Stabilizer bar: Attached to rotor hub trunnion; provides passive gyroscopic stability in all flight conditions
- Pre-coning and under-slinging reduce vibration and provide smooth control response
- Blades flap as a seesaw pair, reducing hub stress versus rigid designs
- Tail rotor: 2-blade, pitch-change via anti-torque pedals

## Hydraulic Systems

- Two independent systems (System 1 and System 2)
- Both systems power cyclic and collective dual servoactuators
- System 1 also powers the tail rotor (anti-torque) servos
- Loss of one system: manageable, controllable
- Loss of both systems: control forces become excessively high — flight must not be attempted

## Flight Controls

### Collective (left hand)
- Lever on left side, moves up/down
- Simultaneously changes pitch on all main rotor blades equally
- Raise collective: increases lift / climbs
- Lower collective: reduces lift / descends
- Every collective change requires simultaneous pedal correction to counteract torque change

### Cyclic (center stick, right hand)
- Tilts the rotor disc via swashplate
- Controls horizontal flight direction and attitude
- Forward: nose pitches down, helicopter accelerates forward
- Aft: nose rises, helicopter decelerates
- Left/Right: lateral movement
- Use small, smooth inputs — over-control causes pilot-induced oscillation

### Anti-Torque Pedals (feet)
- Control tail rotor pitch, which controls heading
- Left pedal: nose yaws left (adds tail rotor pitch, counteracts main rotor torque)
- Right pedal: nose yaws right (reduces tail rotor pitch)
- Must be coordinated with every power and collective change
- Left pedal requires more engine power; right pedal recovers it

### Force Trim System
- Acts on cyclic and pedals
- Maintains control position when released
- Reset by pressing the trim release button

## Crew and Capacity

- Crew: 1 or 2 pilots (dual controls available)
- Passengers: 13–14 in utility config; 15 seats total including pilot
- IFR capability: available; requires 2 instrument-rated pilots

## Notable Characteristics

- High-kinetic-energy rotor stores significant energy in the blades — enables safe autorotational landings
- Doors may be removed in VMC only, symmetrically — door removal shifts CG and reduces VNE
- Approved for VFR day/night; IFR version available
- Torque gauge limit: do not exceed 15% above hover power during acceleration to climb-out
